    The manuscript

    Bahāristān (بهارستان, “Garden of Spring”) is the celebrated 1487 prose-and-verse masterpiece by Nūr al-Dīn ʿAbd al-Raḥmān Jāmī (d. 1492), structured into eight Rawḍas (Gardens) of Sufi parables, royal ethics, wisdom, and literary biography. This royal codex (Bodleian Library, University of Oxford, MS. Elliott 254) was copied in Lahore in 1595 (1003 AH) in master imperial Nastaʿlīq for Mughal Emperor Akbar and later entered the royal library of Emperor Shah Jahan.

    Adorned with delicate gold-painted animal, bird, and floral marginalia throughout, an illuminated ʿunwān frontispiece, and six Mughal court miniatures (including works attributed to imperial masters such as Madhu), the codex later passed through Sir Gore Ouseley—who added a signed 1817 English provenance preface on the opening flyleaves—and John Bardoe Elliott, who bequeathed it to Oxford in 1859.
